# Fixed-N composition search, September 2026

The run `fine-mapping-smart-20260921` completed on 2026-09-23 at 05:06 UTC.
This is a shared-causal analysis. Withdrawn divergent-mode outputs generated by the
old plotting snapshot are archived, not included in the reviewed figures.

Ten candidates crossed five AFR/EUR allocations (50/0, 45/5, 40/10, 35/15 and
25/25 thousand people) with proportional or Covenant-first site recruitment.
Each was sampled with three seeds. Selection used 25 development loci, two
phenotype replicates per architecture, and unconditional causal-capture power.
Overlapping windows could not cross the development/evaluation boundary.

The selected candidate was **45,000 AFR + 5,000 EUR, Covenant-first**. Its actual
recruitment was 46,500 people from Covenant (45,000 AFR and 1,500 EUR) plus 3,500
EUR participants from ANL. MBZUAI contributed none; this is a two-site selected
cohort, not an equal three-site allocation. All arms analyze exactly 50,000 people
except the 150,000-person full federation.

Evaluation used 54 separate loci, 8,080 shared-causal simulation instances and
three sampling seeds. All 24,240 selected-arm fits converged with no execution
errors. Solo/full results are reused for pairing across seeds; they are not three
independent simulation replicates. Confidence intervals resample whole loci.

Selected-minus-Covenant power is **−4.77 percentage points**, with a paired 95%
locus-bootstrap interval of **−5.36 to −4.21**. Selected-minus-ANL is +10.26 points;
selected-minus-MBZUAI is +10.03 points. The selected sampling seeds give 72.76%,
72.86% and 72.98% power. Development power was 73.35%, so the development choice
transferred to the separate evaluation loci without a large observed drop.

The original proportional matched-N controls give 49.29%, 49.86% and 49.60% power
on these same evaluation loci (49.59% pooled). The selected allocation therefore
improves on proportional sampling by 23.28 points, but does not beat Covenant.
Aggregate coverage/FDR do not imply calibration in every design cell. At the
weakest simulated heritability the selected arm's coverage is 95.15% and Covenant's
is 93.65%; this is descriptive and was not the selection objective.

## What the search did not establish

The allocation grid was coarse and excluded CSA altogether. Covenant alone has
47,500 AFR, 1,500 EUR and 1,000 CSA participants. The 45k/5k winner replaces 3,500
Covenant participants with ANL EUR participants. Changes in ancestry-specific
sample size, variant frequency, LD, effects and site-calibrated noise are confounded.
The run does not identify one of these as the entire cause of the remaining gap.
It also does not show that distributed computation loses information: these
composition arms use the same centralized inference driver as their solo controls.

A next search should include Covenant itself as the fallback and allocations near
its composition, for example 47.5k AFR + 2.5k EUR and 48.5k AFR + 1.5k EUR, with
site quotas that retain Covenant's AFR participants before recruiting replacements.
Include CSA-retaining alternatives. If participation from every site is required,
predeclare minimum site contributions rather than adding them after selection.

Select on development data, then use fresh phenotype/effect draws for the final
validation; the current evaluation outcomes have now informed follow-up choices.
Report power, high-confidence yield and calibration, whether or not Covenant is
beaten. Better power than the best solo site remains an empirical question.
